## 1. Route Design Principles

- **Circular loop**: Start and end in Windhoek (vehicle pickup/drop-off)
- **Counter-clockwise**: Windhoek → south to Sossusvlei → west to coast → north along coast/inland → east to Etosha → south back to Windhoek
- **Max ~300–400 km/day on gravel**, arrive at camp before dark
- **2 nights minimum** at major destinations (Sossusvlei, Swakopmund, Etosha) to avoid rushing
- **Omitted for 14 days**: Fish River Canyon (too far south, adds 2+ days), Caprivi Strip (far northeast), Waterberg (nice but non-essential with Etosha)

## 3. Destination Highlights

### Sossusvlei / Deadvlei
- World's tallest sand dunes (Big Daddy ~325m, Dune 45 ~170m)
- Deadvlei: iconic white clay pan with 900-year-old dead camel thorn trees
- Sesriem Canyon: 30m deep, 1km long natural gorge
- Best at sunrise (gates open at first light)

### Swakopmund
- German colonial coastal town, adventure capital of Namibia
- Activities: sandboarding, kayaking with seals (Walvis Bay), Sandwich Harbour 4x4 tour
- Walvis Bay lagoon: flamingos, pelicans, seals
- Good rest stop with restaurants, shops, and laundry

### Cape Cross Seal Colony
- One of the largest Cape fur seal colonies in the world (~200,000 seals)
- Boardwalk along the shore, incredibly noisy and smelly but unforgettable
- Allow 1–2 hours

### Spitzkoppe
- "Matterhorn of Namibia" — dramatic granite inselberg rising from the desert
- Ancient San rock art (guide required)
- Rock Arch — famous natural arch, spectacular at sunset
- World-class stargazing (zero light pollution)
- Community-run campsite, very basic (drop toilets, no showers at sites)

### Damaraland / Twyfelfontein
- Twyfelfontein: UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2,000+ ancient rock engravings (6,000 years old)
- Organ Pipes: columnar basalt formation
- Burnt Mountain: hillside that looks charred
- Petrified Forest: 280-million-year-old fossilized tree trunks
- Desert-adapted elephants: unique population, sometimes seen near dry riverbeds

### Etosha National Park
- Namibia's flagship game reserve, centered around a vast salt pan
- Big game: elephants, lions, leopards, rhinos, giraffes, zebras, oryx, springbok
- Waterholes: floodlit at camps (Okaukuejo famous for nighttime rhino sightings)
- Self-drive safari: well-maintained gravel roads within the park
- 3 main rest camps: Okaukuejo (west), Halali (central), Namutoni (east)

### Okonjima / AfriCat Foundation
- Big cat conservation center between Etosha and Windhoek
- Leopard tracking drives (radio-collar guided, NAD 700/person)
- "Into AfriCat" behind-the-scenes conservation tour
- Good final stop before Windhoek

## 4. Seasonal Considerations

- **Best for wildlife (Etosha)**: May–October (dry season, animals concentrate at waterholes)
- **Best for dunes (Sossusvlei)**: Year-round, but cooler months (May–Sep) more comfortable
- **Rainy season**: November–April — some roads may be impassable, but landscape is green
- **Hottest**: October–December — can exceed 40°C in the desert

## 5. Campsite Pre-Booking Needed

- **Sesriem campsite** (NWR): Book months ahead for peak season
- **Etosha camps** (Okaukuejo, Halali, Namutoni — NWR): Book months ahead
- **Spitzkoppe Community Camp**: Usually available, can book on arrival, but check in peak season
- **Swakopmund**: Multiple private campsites, easier to find availability
- **Damaraland**: Various private campsites around Twyfelfontein

## 3. Camping Logistics

### Campsite Types
1. **NWR campsites** (in national parks): Managed by Namibia Wildlife Resorts. Located in Etosha, Sossusvlei, Waterberg, etc. Facilities vary but usually include ablution blocks, braai pits, sometimes pools and shops.
2. **Private campsites**: Run by lodges and farms throughout the country. Often better facilities and more availability.
3. **Community campsites**: Support local communities, basic facilities, scenic locations.

### NWR Campsite Booking
- Book via [nwr.com.na](https://www.nwr.com.na/) or phone/email
- **Book well in advance** for peak season (May–October) — sites sell out months ahead
- Campsite rates: ~NAD 180–220 per site per night (~EUR 9–11)
- Can sometimes book on arrival if space available, but risky in peak season

### National Park Entry Fees
- **Per person**: NAD 60/day (~EUR 3)
- **Per vehicle**: NAD 10/day (~EUR 0.50)
- Applies to: Etosha, Sossusvlei (Namib-Naukluft), Skeleton Coast, Ai-Ais/Richtersveld

### Wild Camping
- **Not allowed** in national parks or reserves
- **Possible** on private/communal land with landowner permission
- **Recommended**: Stick to designated campsites for safety, facilities, and legality
- Leave no trace — carry all waste out

## 4. Fuel

### Prices (late 2025)
- Petrol 95: ~NAD 20.58/litre (~EUR 1.01/L)
- Diesel 50ppm: ~NAD 20.13/litre (~EUR 0.99/L)

### Key Fuel Stations Along Major Routes
- Windhoek: Multiple stations
- Solitaire: Famous desert outpost with fuel (on the way to Sossusvlei)
- Sesriem: Near Sossusvlei
- Swakopmund/Walvis Bay: Multiple stations
- Uis: Near Spitzkoppe/Brandberg
- Outjo/Otjiwarongo: Gateway to Etosha
- Etosha camps: Fuel available at Okaukuejo and Namutoni
- Khorixas: Damaraland area

### Fuel Strategy
- **Never let the tank drop below half**
- Fill up at every opportunity, especially before remote stretches
- Carry a jerry can (20L) for emergency fuel on remote routes
- Skeleton Coast and remote Damaraland stretches have no fuel for 200+ km
- Most stations accept cards, but carry cash as backup (some rural stations cash-only)

### Estimated Fuel Budget
- ~14 days, ~3,500–4,000 km total driving
- Toyota Hilux: ~12L/100km on gravel
- Total fuel: ~450–500 litres
- Cost: ~NAD 9,000–10,000 (~EUR 450–500)

## 5. Driving Tips

### Road Conditions
- **85%+ of roads are gravel/dirt** — this is normal in Namibia
- Tar roads: B1 (Windhoek–Etosha), B2 (Windhoek–Swakopmund), B4, some others
- Gravel roads: C-roads (good gravel), D-roads (rougher, sometimes 4x4 only)

### Essential Driving Rules
1. **Drive on the LEFT** (same as UK)
2. **Max 80 km/h on gravel** — often slower is safer
3. **Never drive after dark** — animals on the road, no road markings, potholes invisible
4. **Reduce tyre pressure** 10–20% on gravel for better traction (re-inflate on tar)
5. Allow **more time than Google Maps suggests** — gravel roads are slow
6. Aim for **max 300–400 km/day**, arrive at camp before dark

### Common Hazards
- **Corrugated gravel**: Causes vibration, loosens bolts; slow down
- **Soft sand**: Momentum is key; don't brake suddenly
- **Tyre blowouts**: Very common; carry 2 spare tyres and know how to change them
- **Animals on roads**: Warthogs, kudu, oryx especially at dawn/dusk
- **River crossings**: Check depth first; only cross in 4x4 with low range

### Emergency Preparedness
- Carry: 2 spare tyres, tyre repair kit, jack, tow rope
- Carry: 10–20L extra water, extra fuel
- Have offline maps (Maps.me or Tracks4Africa app)
- Carry a paper map as backup
- Note emergency numbers and nearest town with a hospital
- Some rental companies include satellite phones — highly recommended for remote areas

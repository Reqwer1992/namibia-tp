## 1. General Safety Assessment

Namibia is **one of the safest countries in Africa** for tourists. The US State Department rates it Level 2 ("Exercise increased caution"). Self-drive tourism is well-established and thousands of visitors do it safely each year.

**However**:
- Crime exists, especially in **Windhoek** (mugging, car break-ins, bag snatching)
- **Traffic accidents** are the biggest danger — gravel roads + speed + animals = risk
- **Remoteness** means help can be far away if something goes wrong

---

## 2. Crime & Personal Safety

### Windhoek
- Mugging and bag-snatching occur in the city centre, even during daytime
- Avoid walking alone after dark, especially in the Independence Avenue / Katutura areas
- Don't carry valuables visibly; leave passport/documents locked in the vehicle or campsite safe
- Car break-ins common in parking lots — never leave anything visible in the car

### On the Road
- Don't stop for hitchhikers on remote roads
- Avoid roadside rest stops where robberies have occurred
- Keep doors locked and windows up when driving through towns
- Park in well-lit, busy areas

### At Campsites
- Generally very safe, especially NWR and established private campsites
- Lock valuables in the vehicle at night
- Don't leave food out — baboons and jackals will take it

## 3. Road & Driving Dangers

This is the **single biggest risk** for tourists in Namibia.

### Key Dangers
1. **Excessive speed on gravel**: Most accidents are single-vehicle rollovers from speeding on gravel. Max 80 km/h, often slower.
2. **Tyre blowouts**: Very common on gravel/sharp rocks. Carry 2 spares, know how to change a tyre.
3. **Animals on roads**: Kudu, warthogs, oryx, donkeys. Especially dangerous at dawn/dusk/night. Kudu can jump into your windshield.
4. **Night driving**: No street lights, no road markings, animals invisible. **Never drive after dark.**
5. **Soft sand**: Some roads have deep sand patches. Keep momentum, don't brake suddenly.
6. **Fatigue**: Long distances on corrugated gravel are exhausting. Limit driving to 4–6 hours/day.
7. **No cell coverage**: In remote areas, you're on your own if something happens.

### What to Do If You Break Down
- Stay with your vehicle (it's easier to find than a person on foot)
- Use hazard triangle (should be in the vehicle)
- Try to flag down passing vehicles
- If you have signal, call your rental company's 24/7 line
- Carry at least 10L water per person for emergencies

## 4. Wildlife & Nature Risks

### Dangerous Animals
- **Elephants**: Can be aggressive, especially in Damaraland and Etosha. Keep distance (50m+), never block their path, turn off engine if they approach.
- **Hippos**: Along rivers in the Caprivi/Zambezi region (not on this itinerary). Most dangerous animal in Africa.
- **Snakes**: Puff adders, cobras, black mambas exist. Watch where you step, especially at night. Shake out shoes in the morning.
- **Scorpions**: Check shoes and clothing left outside. Don't walk barefoot at night.
- **Spiders**: Some large species; mostly harmless.
- **Baboons**: Will steal food aggressively. Never feed them. Keep food locked away.

### Environmental Hazards
- **Extreme heat**: Can exceed 40°C in the desert (Oct–Mar). Drink 5L+ water per day. Avoid midday sun.
- **Sunburn**: High altitude + clear skies = intense UV. Wear SPF 50+, hat, and long sleeves.
- **Dehydration**: Onset can be rapid. Symptoms: headache, dizziness, dark urine. Drink water constantly.
- **Flash floods**: Dry riverbeds can flood suddenly during rainy season (Nov–Apr). Never camp in a dry riverbed.
- **Dust storms**: Can reduce visibility to near zero. Pull over and wait.

## 5. Health Precautions

### Malaria
- **Risk areas**: Northern Namibia (Etosha, Caprivi, Kavango). This itinerary passes through.
- **Prophylaxis**: Malarone recommended. Start 1–2 days before, take daily, continue 7 days after leaving risk area.
- **Prevention**: DEET repellent, long sleeves at dusk/dawn, keep tent zipped.
- **Symptoms**: Fever, chills, headache, muscle pain. Can appear up to 4 weeks after exposure. Seek medical help immediately.

### Water
- Tap water safe in Windhoek and Swakopmund
- Elsewhere, use bottled or purified water
- Always carry 10L+ in the vehicle

### Nearest Hospitals Along the Route
- Windhoek: Windhoek Central Hospital, Mediclinic Windhoek
- Swakopmund: Cottage Medi-Clinic Swakopmund
- Otjiwarongo: Otjiwarongo State Hospital
- Outjo: Outjo State Hospital
- Etosha area: Basic clinics at park camps; nearest hospital in Tsumeb or Outjo

## 6. Scams & Tourist Traps

### Common Scams
1. **Fake charity collectors**: People claiming to collect for orphanages/schools. Often not legitimate.
2. **Diamond sellers**: Buying unregistered diamonds/gems is illegal and can result in arrest.
3. **Inflated prices**: Roadside vendors and some markets quote wildly inflated prices. Haggle.
4. **Fake police**: Rarely, but reported — real police carry ID and won't ask for cash bribes on the spot.
5. **Car window smash-and-grab**: In Windhoek traffic. Keep windows up, doors locked, bags hidden.

### Tourist Traps
- Overpriced activity operators (especially in Swakopmund). Compare prices between companies.
- "Semi-precious stones" sold at inflated prices at roadside stalls.

## 7. Things NOT to Do

1. **Don't drive after dark** — animals, no markings, no lights. This is the #1 rule.
2. **Don't speed on gravel** — max 80 km/h, ideally slower.
3. **Don't drive off-road** in national parks or desert areas — it's illegal, destroys fragile ecosystems, and leaves tracks visible for decades.
4. **Don't fly drones** in national parks (Etosha, Sossusvlei, Skeleton Coast) — strictly prohibited. Flying anywhere requires a permit from the DCA, applied 60+ days in advance.
5. **Don't approach or feed wildlife** — keep distance, especially from elephants and baboons.
6. **Don't walk in Windhoek after dark** — use taxis or your own vehicle.
7. **Don't leave valuables visible** in the car — ever.
8. **Don't pick up hitchhikers** on remote roads.
9. **Don't camp in dry riverbeds** — flash flood risk in rainy season.
10. **Don't underestimate distances** — Namibia is huge. Plan rest days.
11. **Don't litter** — Leave No Trace. Namibia's pristine landscapes are its main attraction.
12. **Don't photograph people without permission** — it's rude and can cause conflict. Ask first, especially with Himba communities.
13. **Don't buy diamonds or uncut gems** from street vendors — it's illegal.

- **EU Delegation in Namibia**: +264 (0) 63 200 2202
- **German Embassy Windhoek**: +264 61 273 100 (Germany often handles Latvian consular matters in countries without Latvian representation)

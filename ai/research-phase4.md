## 1. Budget Breakdown (2 persons, 14 days)

### Flights
- BA return LHR → WDH (via JNB): GBP 650–1,200 per person
- **Budget estimate**: GBP 1,600 for 2 (~EUR 1,870)

### Visa
- E-visa: NAD 1,600 per person × 2 = NAD 3,200 (~EUR 160)

### Vehicle Rental (14 days)
- Namibia2Go budget option (15+ day rate): NAD 2,376/day × 14 = NAD 33,264 (~EUR 1,640)
- Asco Car Hire low season: ~EUR 105/day × 14 = ~EUR 1,470
- **Budget estimate**: EUR 1,500–1,800 (including insurance)

### Fuel
- ~3,200 km at ~12L/100km = ~384L
- Diesel at ~NAD 20/L = NAD 7,680 (~EUR 380)
- **Budget estimate**: EUR 380–500

### National Park Fees
- Etosha: 3 days × (NAD 150/person × 2 + NAD 50/vehicle) = NAD 1,050
- Sossusvlei/Namib-Naukluft: 2 days × (NAD 150 × 2 + NAD 50) = NAD 700
- Skeleton Coast (Cape Cross): 1 day = NAD 350
- Spitzkoppe community fee: ~NAD 200
- Other parks: ~NAD 400
- **Total park fees**: ~NAD 2,700 (~EUR 135)

### Campsites (13 nights)
- NWR campsites (Etosha, Sesriem): ~NAD 300–600/site/night × 5 nights = ~NAD 2,250
- Private/community campsites: ~NAD 200–400/site × 8 nights = ~NAD 2,400
- **Total camping**: ~NAD 4,650 (~EUR 230)

### Food & Groceries
- Self-catering mostly: ~NAD 400–600/day for 2 people
- Budget for 14 days: ~NAD 7,000 (~EUR 345)
- Includes: a few restaurant meals in Swakopmund and Windhoek

### Activities
- Cape Cross entry: ~NAD 120/person = NAD 240
- Twyfelfontein guided tour: ~NAD 120/person = NAD 240
- Walvis Bay kayaking/Sandwich Harbour: ~NAD 1,500/person = NAD 3,000
- Okonjima leopard tracking: ~NAD 700/person = NAD 1,400
- Other small entries/tips: ~NAD 500
- **Total activities**: ~NAD 5,380 (~EUR 265)

### SIM Card & Data
- MTC SIM: NAD 10
- 30-day plan (18GB data, 300 min, 150 SMS): NAD 290 × 2 = NAD 580
- **Total**: ~NAD 590 (~EUR 30)

### Miscellaneous
- Water purchases, firewood, tips, souvenirs: ~NAD 2,000 (~EUR 100)

### Money-Saving Tips
1. **Cook your own food** — supermarkets in Windhoek and Swakopmund have good prices. Pasta, rice, canned goods, braai meat are cheap.
2. **Travel in low season** (Nov–Jun) — vehicle rental drops 30–40%.
3. **Book vehicle early** — early bird discounts from most companies.
4. **Skip expensive activities** — Sandwich Harbour tours and hot air balloons are pricey. The free/cheap sights (dunes, Etosha, stargazing) are the highlights.
5. **Carry water from towns** — buying water at remote campsites costs more.
6. **Use MTC data wisely** — download offline maps and content in town. Signal is sparse in remote areas anyway.

## 2. Connectivity

### Mobile Network
- **MTC** is the best network in Namibia with widest coverage
- Buy SIM at Windhoek Airport on arrival
- Best tourist plan: 18GB / 30 days for NAD 290 (~EUR 14)
- Coverage: good in towns and along main roads; patchy to non-existent in remote areas (Skeleton Coast, deep Damaraland, parts of Etosha)

### Navigation
- **Offline maps are essential**: Download Maps.me or Google Maps offline before leaving town
- **Tracks4Africa** app is the gold standard for Namibia navigation (paid, ~EUR 30)
- Paper map as backup (buy in Windhoek)

## 3. Practical Tips

### Best Time to Visit
- **Dry season (May–October)**: Best for wildlife (Etosha), comfortable temperatures, no rain. Peak tourist season, higher prices.
- **Shoulder (April, November)**: Good compromise — fewer crowds, reasonable weather
- **Wet season (December–March)**: Hot, some roads impassable, but green landscapes and bird watching. Cheapest.

### Currency & Payments
- **Namibian Dollar (NAD)**, pegged 1:1 to South African Rand (ZAR). Both accepted everywhere.
- Cards widely accepted in towns but **carry cash for**:
  - Remote fuel stations
  - Park fees at gates
  - Community campsites
  - Tips and small purchases
- ATMs available in Windhoek, Swakopmund, Otjiwarongo, Outjo, and other towns.

### Water
- Tap water is generally safe in towns (Windhoek, Swakopmund)
- Carry 10L+ drinking water in the vehicle at all times
- In remote areas, buy bottled water or use purification tablets
- Budget: ~5L per person per day in the desert

### Electricity
- Namibia uses Type D and Type M plugs (round 3-pin, same as South Africa)
- Bring a **South African adapter** (or universal adapter)
- Many campsites have power points; some don't
- Bring a car charger for phones/GPS

### Photography
- Bring a camera with decent zoom for wildlife (200mm+ ideal for Etosha)
- Binoculars are essential for game viewing
- Dust is everywhere — bring a lens cloth and protective bag for camera gear
- Golden hour (sunrise/sunset) is spectacular at the dunes and Spitzkoppe

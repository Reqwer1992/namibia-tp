# Phase 6 Research: Early March Conditions in Namibia

## 1. Weather & Temperatures by Region

Early March is the tail end of Namibia's rainy season ("green season," November–March/April).

| Region | High °C | Low °C | Rain mm | Rainy Days | Malaria Risk |
|--------|---------|--------|---------|------------|--------------|
| Windhoek | 30 | 16 | ~80 | ~8 | Very low |
| Sossusvlei/Sesriem | 29–34 | 15–20 | 15–39 | ~3 | None |
| Swakopmund/Coast | 20–25 | 15 | ~0 | 0 | None |
| Damaraland | ~29 | ~17 | ~50–60 | ~5–6 | Low |
| Etosha | 32–34 | 19 | ~80 | ~6–8 | Low–moderate |
| Northern Namibia | 31–35 | 17–20 | 80–100+ | ~8–10 | HIGH |

**General pattern**: Rain falls as short, intense afternoon/evening thunderstorms. Mornings are usually clear. Rain clears dust from the air, leaving exceptional visibility for photography.

## 2. Road Conditions

### B-roads (tarred)
Generally passable year-round. Heavy rains can create potholes and some river fords may flood temporarily. Major routes B1 (Windhoek–Etosha), B2 (Windhoek–Swakopmund), B6 are generally fine.

### C-roads (major gravel)
- **C14 (Kuiseb Pass, Sesriem → Swakopmund)**: Gravel with steep sections (1:9 gradient), corrugations, tight corners. Usually open year-round but after heavy rain becomes slippery and sandy/muddy sections worsen. Crosses the Kuiseb River via a ford. 4x4 recommended.
- C-roads generally deteriorate after heavy rain with washouts and mud.

### D-roads (minor gravel/dirt)
- D-roads in Damaraland pass through dry riverbeds (Ugab, Huab) that can flood. After heavy rain, some become impassable. 4x4 essential.
- **D1918 (to Spitzkoppe)**: Good in dry conditions, deteriorates after rain. 4x4 recommended after rain.

### River crossings
Ephemeral rivers (Kuiseb, Swakop, Omaruru, Ugab, Hoarusib, Hoanib, Khumib) can be bone-dry for months then become raging torrents within hours. **Largest floods most frequently occur in February and March.** Almost every year, people and vehicles are swept away.

**Rules**: If water is deeper than 50 cm (4x4), do not cross. Never cross at night. Never cross if water is flowing fast or muddy.

## 3. Etosha in Wet Season

### How game viewing changes
- Animals **do not need to congregate at waterholes** — water is scattered everywhere. Classic large-herd waterhole viewing is reduced.
- Wildlife **disperses** across open plains and bush. Dense vegetation makes spotting harder.
- Game viewing **improves as March progresses** as rainfall tapers off.

### What you WILL see
- **Baby animals**: Springbok, zebra, wildebeest calves born Jan/Feb
- **Up to 1 million flamingos** at Etosha's salt pan and Fischer's Pan
- **340+ bird species**, many migratory
- Green, photogenic landscapes with dramatic skies

### Best areas in March
- **Namutoni (east)**: Best for birding. Closest to Fischer's Pan — transforms into seasonal lake (Jan–Apr), haven for flamingos, waterbirds. Good for cheetah.
- **Okaukuejo (south/central)**: Floodlit waterhole still active, increasingly so as March progresses. Best for nighttime game viewing.
- **Halali (central)**: Thicker vegetation; better for leopard, rhino, elephant.

### Is 3 nights worth it?
Yes — for different reasons than dry season. Baby animals, spectacular birding (flamingos at Fischer's Pan), green landscapes, dramatic thunderstorm skies, fewer tourists.

## 4. Sossusvlei in March

- **Heat**: 29–35°C daytime. Start climbing dunes at dawn. By mid-morning sand is uncomfortable for bare feet.
- **Access road**: 60 km tarred road from gate to 2x4 parking is fine year-round. Final 5 km to vlei requires 4x4 (always).
- **Dunes**: Not affected by rain. If rain has fallen, the vlei may hold water creating stunning reflections — arguably Sossusvlei at its most beautiful.
- **Rainfall**: ~15 mm average in March = mostly dry, sunny days with perhaps 3 rainy days in the month.
- **Carry minimum 3L water per person per hike.**

## 5. Flash Flood Risk

**Very serious in February–March.** Almost every year, people, vehicles, and animals are swept away.

### Most at-risk areas
- **Northern Namibia**: Kunene, Omusati, Oshana, Ohangwena, Zambezi. Cuvelai drainage system especially flood-prone. In 2025, 16 lives lost.
- **Ephemeral river crossings**: Kuiseb, Swakop, Omaruru, Ugab rivers.
- **Windhoek**: City itself floods — 21 flood incidents in Jan–Mar 2025.
- **Damaraland**: Ugab River area near Brandberg declared unsafe during heavy flows.

### Safety rules
- Never camp in a dry riverbed
- Never cross a flowing river at night
- Rain upstream (out of sight) can cause flooding hours later at your location

## 6. Malaria Risk

### Risk level in March
Peak transmission window (November–April). Stagnant water creates ideal mosquito breeding.

### 2026 outbreak alert
Namibia's Ministry of Health issued a malaria outbreak alert in early 2026. First 4 weeks of 2026: **8,760 confirmed cases — 68% increase** over same period in 2025. Concentrated in northern/northeastern regions.

### Risk by area on itinerary
- **HIGH**: Caprivi Strip, Kavango, Kunene River (not on itinerary)
- **LOW–MODERATE**: Etosha National Park — elevated in wet season. Given 2026 outbreak, prophylaxis strongly recommended.
- **VERY LOW / NONE**: Windhoek, Swakopmund, Sossusvlei, southern/coastal areas

### Recommendation
**Take prophylaxis (Malarone preferred)**. Doxycycline causes sun sensitivity — a drawback in Namibia. Malarone has fewer side effects but costs more. Consult GP or travel health clinic 6–8 weeks before departure.

Verify rooftop tent has mesh screens with rental company. Use DEET repellent at dusk/dawn.

## 7. Tourist Crowds & Pricing

March is **low season**. Significantly fewer tourists.

- Accommodation: up to 50% cheaper than peak (Jun–Oct), typically 10–30% discount
- Campsites (NWR): relatively stable year-round (government-regulated)
- Vehicle rental: lower rates, better availability
- Flights: generally cheaper
- Last-minute bookings often possible

## 8. Pros & Cons of March Travel

### Pros
- Green, lush landscapes with wildflowers
- Baby animals (springbok, zebra, wildebeest calves)
- Up to 1 million flamingos at Etosha
- Migratory bird species present (340+ species)
- Significantly fewer tourists — quieter, more personal
- Lower prices (accommodation, flights, vehicles)
- Photography: dramatic skies, dust-free air, green landscapes, water reflections
- Sossusvlei vlei may hold water (stunning reflections)

### Cons
- Heat: 30–35°C inland, up to 40°C in Kalahari
- Afternoon thunderstorms can disrupt plans
- Some gravel roads deteriorate; D-roads may become impassable
- River crossings can flood (flash flood risk)
- Reduced big-game visibility at Etosha waterholes (animals disperse)
- Elevated malaria risk in northern Namibia (2026 outbreak)
- More mosquitoes and insects
- Some remote lodges close (most major ones stay open)

### Overall assessment
Early March is a legitimate and rewarding option. Best for photography, birding, and budget. Main trade-offs: reduced classic Etosha waterhole viewing, heat, road caution, and malaria precautions.

## 9. Vehicle Rental in Rainy Season

- **4x4 essential** — even more so than dry season
- Ensure good tyre tread depth — check at pickup
- Some rental agreements **exclude water damage** — read fine print
- Full insurance (including underbody/tyre damage) strongly recommended
- Never park/camp in dry riverbeds
- Verify rooftop tent has mosquito mesh screens
- Carry waterproof bags for gear — afternoon thunderstorms can soak exposed equipment
- Allow extra time for journeys — wet/degraded gravel is slower

## 10. Daylight Hours

Near the autumnal equinox (March 20). Days slightly longer than 12 hours.

| Date | Sunrise | Sunset | Daylight |
|------|---------|--------|----------|
| Mar 1 | 06:47 | 19:19 | 12h 32m |
| Mar 7 | 06:50 | 19:14 | 12h 24m |
| Mar 14 | 06:52 | 19:09 | 12h 17m |
| Mar 20 | 06:54 | 19:04 | ~12h 10m |

Ample daylight for activities. Etosha gates close at sunset — plan last game drive accordingly. Sunset takes ~26 minutes (extended golden hour for photography).

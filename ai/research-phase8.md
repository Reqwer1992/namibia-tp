# Phase 8 Research: Road Conditions & How to Check Them

## Official Sources

### Roads Authority of Namibia (RA)
- **No live road condition map or real-time closure reports** on the website
- Must contact directly or check social media for closure announcements
- Website: www.ra.org.na
- Phone: +264 61 284 7000
- Email: pr@ra.org.na
- **Facebook**: "Roads Authority RA Namibia" — best official source for closure notices
- Twitter/X: @RANamibia

### Namibia Flood and Drought Monitor
- Operational early warning system (Princeton Climate Institute / University of Southampton)
- Current precipitation, soil moisture, runoff data
- 10-day flood forecasts updated daily
- URL: https://hydrology.soton.ac.uk/apps/nam_app/
- Useful for assessing flood risk along your route before departing each morning

### Namibia Meteorological Service
- Issues storm and flood warnings
- February 2026: warnings for severe thunderstorms in central-north, Zambezi, Kavango, Otjozondjupa, and Omaheke regions

### NamWater Hydrological Services
- Monitors river levels and catchment flooding
- Flood alerts for Cuvelai-Etosha Basin, Okavango River, Kuiseb catchment, Hardap-Mariental area
- Website: www.namwater.com.na

## Community & Crowdsourced Sources

### Tracks4Africa (T4A) — Best Overall Tool
- Detailed offline GPS maps of Namibia
- Road surface types and quality ratings
- Quarterly updates
- Cost: ~USD 10–15
- Limitation: quarterly updates, so won't reflect last week's washout

### iOverlander
- Crowdsourced app/website
- Only allows reports for roads that are **not passable** (2+ hour reroute)

## On-the-Ground Methods

1. **Campsite hosts / lodge reception** — single best resource. Ask every evening about tomorrow's route.
2. **Fuel station attendants** — see every vehicle, hear from truckers. Especially useful at Solitaire, Uis, Khorixas, Kamanjab.
3. **Other travelers** — people coming from the opposite direction know exactly what to expect.
4. **Rental company** — call them if unsure. They track conditions because their vehicles are affected.
5. **Park gates** (Etosha, Namib-Naukluft) — ask gate staff and NWR camp reception about internal road conditions.

## Current Conditions — February 2026 (Rainy Season)

### B1 and B2 (tar roads)
- Generally good
- B1 Aris River Bridge (near Windhoek) collapsed March 2025 — temporary single-lane bypass installed, permanent repair was estimated at 7 months. Confirm status before departure.
- Potholes reported on tar roads after recent rains

### C24 (Windhoek → Sesriem)
- Expect corrugation and possible washouts
- Tsauchab River has flooded and reached Sossusvlei during recent rains
- River crossings may be temporarily impassable
- Ask at Solitaire or Sesriem about conditions

### C14 (Sesriem → Swakopmund via Kuiseb Pass) — HIGH RISK
- Named as one of the roads that became **impassable** during 2025 rainy season
- Heavy corrugation, loose sand/stone, protruding rocks between Kries se Rus and Kuiseb Canyon
- "Major slippery parts" reported March 2025
- Lower Kuiseb Catchment is a flood-prone area
- **Alternative**: Return to B1 via Rehoboth, take B2 to Swakopmund (longer but fully tarred)

### D1918 (to Spitzkoppe)
- Generally passable with 4x4
- Two known bad sections: first 40 km from Usakos side, and 30–40 km before Henties Bay
- Watch for soft sand patches after rain

### D-roads in Damaraland — CAUTION
- D2612 (Sorris-Sorris to Twyfelfontein): structural damage including gravel loss and sand accumulation
- MR76 (Uis to Kamanjab): impassable sections with mud-filled river crossings
- M126 (Khorixas to Bergsig, Palmwag to Kamanjab): washed out and flooded
- No constructed river crossings on D-roads — flash floods come and go quickly
- Get conditions from Uis or Khorixas before venturing onto D-roads

### Etosha National Park
- Most roads are graded gravel
- Anderson Gate → Okaukuejo and Von Lindequist Gate → Namutoni are paved
- Expect deep ruts and large puddles in February
- Some areas may be temporarily closed
- Main loop Okaukuejo–Halali–Namutoni should be passable
